                        Each node is a verified Facebook page and each edge
                        represents a mutual "like" between two pages. The pages
                        belong to categories such as politicians, companies,
                        TV shows, athletes, etc.

   Key Statistics:
   +--------------------------+----------------+
   | Metric                   | Value          |
   +--------------------------+----------------+
   | Total Nodes (N)          | 22,470         |
   | Total Edges              | 171,002        |
   | Graph Type               | Undirected     |
   | Average Degree           | 15.22          |
   | Network Density          | 0.000677       |
   | Is Connected             | True (1 comp.) |
   +--------------------------+----------------+

   The entire graph is a single connected component, meaning every node can

2. METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

   The project follows these steps as per the professor's instructions:

   Step 1: Pick N nodes from a social media dataset.
           We used all N = 22,470 nodes in the Facebook MUSAE dataset.

   Step 2: Define undirected links.
           Two nodes are linked if a friend/follower relationship exists
           between them (mutual likes between Facebook pages). The graph
           is undirected.

   Step 3: Extract K-distance induced neighbourhood subgraphs.
           For each of the 22,470 nodes, we extracted the K-distance induced
           neighbourhood subgraph. This is the subgraph formed by ALL nodes
           within K hops of a given node, along with all edges between them.

   Step 4: Feature Engineering (20-dimensional dataset).
           For each node's K-neighbourhood subgraph:
             - Compute the degree of every node WITHIN the induced subgraph
             - Sort degrees in descending order
             - Take the top 20 degree values as the feature vector
           This produces a dataset of shape: 22,470 x 20

   Step 5: PCA Analysis.
           Apply PCA (after StandardScaler normalization) to the 20-dimensional
           dataset and determine how many principal components are needed to
           capture 60%, 80%, and 90% of the total variance.


3. CHOICE OF K (CRITICAL DESIGN DECISION)
================================================================================

   The professor suggested K = 10. However, this is impractical for the
   Facebook dataset due to the "small-world" property of social networks.

   We verified this by sampling 20 nodes and measuring their neighbourhood
   sizes at K = 1, 2, and 3:

   Observation:
   - At K = 1: Neighbourhood sizes range from ~2 to ~50 nodes
                (only direct neighbours)
   - At K = 2: Neighbourhood sizes range from ~3 to ~5,917 nodes
                (up to 26% of the entire graph!)
   - At K = 3: Neighbourhood sizes would cover nearly the ENTIRE graph
                for most nodes (approaching 22,470)

   Decision: We used K = 2 because:
   (a) K = 3 or higher would result in almost identical subgraphs for all
       nodes (covering the whole connected component), making the features
       indistinguishable.
   (b) K = 2 provides meaningful variation in neighbourhood sizes (3 to 5,917).
   (c) K = 1 would give very small subgraphs with few nodes, potentially
       not enough to extract 20 meaningful degree values.

   This choice is directly related to the small-world property of the network.


4. WHAT IS THE "SMALL-WORLD" PROPERTY?
================================================================================

   A "small-world" network is a type of graph where most nodes can be reached
   from every other node in a very small number of steps (hops), even though
   the total number of nodes may be very large.

   Key characteristics of small-world networks:

   (a) SHORT AVERAGE PATH LENGTH:
       The average shortest path between any two nodes is surprisingly small.
       For social networks with millions of users, this is typically 4-6 hops.
       This is the famous "six degrees of separation" phenomenon.

   (b) HIGH CLUSTERING COEFFICIENT:
       Nodes tend to form tightly-knit groups. If person A is friends with
       B and C, then B and C are also likely friends. This creates dense
       local clusters.

   (c) DEGREE DISTRIBUTION FOLLOWS POWER LAW:
       A few "hub" nodes have very high degree (many connections), while
       the vast majority of nodes have low degree. The log-log degree
       distribution plot in our analysis confirms this.

   For our Facebook dataset (22,470 nodes, avg degree 15.22):
   - Just 2 hops covers up to 26% of the entire graph
   - 3 hops covers almost the entire graph

   This means information (or influence) can spread extremely rapidly
   through the network. It also means that K-neighbourhood subgraphs
   at K >= 2 are very large and heavily overlapping.


5. PCA RESULTS
================================================================================

   After standardizing the 22,470 x 20 feature matrix (zero mean, unit
   variance per feature), we applied PCA with all 20 components.

   Results:

   +-----+-------------------+--------------------+
   | PC  | Individual Var %  | Cumulative Var %   |
   +-----+-------------------+--------------------+
   | PC1 |           98.5%   |           98.5%    |
   | PC2 |            0.8%   |           99.3%    |
   | PC3 |            0.2%   |           99.5%    |
   | ... |            ...    |            ...     |
   +-----+-------------------+--------------------+

   Variance Thresholds:
   +---------------------+-------------------+
   | Threshold           | PCs Required      |
   +---------------------+-------------------+
   | 60% variance        | 1 PC              |
   | 80% variance        | 1 PC              |
   | 90% variance        | 1 PC              |
   +---------------------+-------------------+

   PC1 alone explains 98.5% of the total variance.
   PC1 + PC2 together explain 99.3%.

   This means only 1 principal component is sufficient to capture
   60%, 80%, AND 90% of the variance in our 20-dimensional dataset.


6. INTERPRETATION OF RESULTS
================================================================================

   The finding that PC1 captures 98.5% of variance is SIGNIFICANT.
   It is NOT an error — it reveals an important structural property
   of the Facebook network.

   WHY does this happen?

   (a) FEATURE HOMOGENEITY:
       The 20 features (top-20 degrees in the K=2 neighbourhood) are
       almost perfectly correlated with each other. When a node's
       neighbourhood is large, ALL 20 top-degree values are high.
       When it's small, ALL are low. They scale together.

   (b) HUB NODE DOMINANCE:
       The same hub nodes (with highest degree in the full graph) appear
       in the K=2 neighbourhoods of thousands of different nodes.
       So the "top 20 degrees" are dominated by the same hubs across
       almost all neighborhoods.

   (c) OVERLAPPING NEIGHBOURHOODS:
       Due to the small-world property, K=2 neighbourhoods of different
       nodes share massive portions of their members. Two nodes that
       are 2 hops apart have nearly identical K=2 neighbourhoods.

   (d) ONE DOMINANT DIMENSION:
       Since all 20 features essentially measure the same thing
       ("how large and well-connected is my neighbourhood?"), a single
       principal component capturing the "overall neighbourhood size/
       connectivity" is sufficient to explain nearly all variance.

8. KEY TAKEAWAYS / CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

   1. The Facebook MUSAE dataset exhibits strong SMALL-WORLD properties:
      the network is fully connected, and K=2 neighbourhoods cover up to
      26% of the entire graph (up to 5,917 out of 22,470 nodes).

   2. Due to the small-world property, K=10 (as suggested by the professor)
      is impractical for this dataset. Even K=3 would cover the entire
      graph. We used K=2, which provides meaningful variation.

   3. The 20-dimensional feature dataset (top-20 degrees per K=2
      neighbourhood) has extremely high inter-feature correlation
      because the neighbourhoods are large and overlapping, and the
      same hub nodes dominate the degree rankings across all
      neighbourhoods.

   4. PCA reveals that ONLY 1 PRINCIPAL COMPONENT is sufficient to
      capture 98.5% of the variance. This is a direct consequence
      of the small-world property and feature construction method.

   5. This result is not wrong or unexpected — it is a genuine and
      important finding about the structure of social networks. The
      high variance captured by PC1 reflects the fact that the network
      has a single dominant structural dimension: the "neighbourhood
      size / hub connectivity" axis.

   6. For more differentiated PCA results (more PCs needed for 90%),
      one could:
      - Use K=1 instead of K=2 (smaller, more diverse neighbourhoods)
      - Use different features (normalized degrees, clustering
        coefficients, centrality measures)
      - Use a larger, sparser dataset (like the Twitter dataset
        in the reference notebook with 5.3M nodes)
      - Use degree distribution histograms instead of raw top-K degrees
